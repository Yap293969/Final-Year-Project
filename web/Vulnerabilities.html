Hidden weak spots
and why updates matter.

Most phishing tricks you. But now and then a message carries something that tricks your app instead, by using a mistake hidden in its code. Here's what those mistakes are, six real ones, and the easy habit that blocks them.

01 What is a weak spot?

Apps are written by people, and people make mistakes. Some mistakes let an attacker make the app do something it shouldn't. Security experts call that a vulnerability, and give each known one an ID called a CVE.

    02 Two kinds of trap

    A scam message can go after two different things. Knowing which helps you know what protects you.

    Most phishing

    It tricks you

    A fake page, a scary story, a request for your password or code. No weak spot is needed: it works on how people feel.

    What protects you: Stop, Check, Ask — the main guide.

    Rarer

    It tricks your app

    A file, picture or email built to hit a weak spot in an app. Sometimes you only need to open it, and once in a while not even that.

    What protects you: keeping your apps and phone updated.

    About our checker. It reads the words of an email and the shape of a link, so it looks for the first kind. It doesn't open files or scan them for weak spots, which is why updates still matter.

    03 Six real cases

    Each of these reached people through a message, a file or a web page. All six are on CISA's list of weak spots attackers really used, and all six were stopped by an update.

      04 What to do: keep the patches coming

      You can't spot a hidden weak spot yourself. You don't need to. The fix comes to you as an update — you just have to let it in.

      Easy habits

      • Turn on automatic updates on your phone, computer and apps.
      • When an app or browser asks to restart, say yes soon. That's when the fix goes in.
      • Update apps you rarely use too, like file openers and old games.
      • Phone update waiting? Install it within a few days, with the phone charging.

      Stop and ask first

      • Opening a file, ZIP or document you weren't expecting.
      • A document that tells you to "Enable editing" or "Enable content".
      • An "update" offered inside a message or pop-up. Update from the app store or settings instead.
      • Apps from outside the official app store.

      Remember Updates + Stop, Check, Ask = most traps blocked.
