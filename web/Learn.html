01 What is phishing?

Phishing is when someone pretends to be a person or company you trust — a bank, a delivery company, your school, a game, even a friend — to trick you into clicking a link, typing a password, sharing a code or sending money.

The name comes from fishing: the scammer throws out bait to thousands of people and waits for someone to bite. They don't need everyone. They only need you, once.

  1. The bait

    A message that looks real

    A logo, a familiar name, a believable story. It is built to look like something you'd expect.

  2. The hook

    Something to do, right now

    “Log in”, “claim”, “verify”, “pay a small fee”, “scan this QR code”.

  3. The catch

    What they really want

    Your password, your one-time code (OTP), your card details, your money, or your account.

It's not only email. The same trick comes by SMS (smishing), phone calls (vishing), QR codes (quishing), social media DMs, chat apps and in-game chat. Same bait, different pond.

02 Why it works on anyone

Scammers rarely hack your phone. They hack your feelings. A strong feeling makes you act before you think — and that's exactly the moment they want.

Being smart doesn't protect you. Noticing the feeling does.

  • ⏱

    Hurry

    No time to stop and check.

    Reply within 24 hours or lose access.
  • ⚠

    Fear

    Something bad will happen if you don't act.

    Your account will be deleted.
  • 🎁

    Reward

    Something free, rare or too good.

    You've won! Claim your prize now.
  • 👀

    Curiosity

    You just have to see it.

    Is this you in this video??
  • 🏫

    Trust

    It seems to come from someone in charge, or someone you know.

    Hi, it's your teacher / your bank / me, new number.

Remember Strong feeling + a request to act fast = slow down.

03 Red flags at first sight

You don't need to be an expert. Give every unexpected message a 5-second check:

  1. Who sent it? Does the name match the address or number?
  2. Is it rushing or scaring me?
  3. Is it too good to be true?
  4. Where does the link really go? (Part 04 shows how.)
  5. Is it asking for a password, a code, or money?

04 Read the link like a pro

A link can show any brand name it likes. What matters is who really owns it. Find the first single / after https://, and read the words just before it. That's the owner.

https://login.mybank.com.my/account/settings
  • how to connect
  • a part the owner can name anything
  • the real owner
  • which page on that website

Who really owns this link?

Guess first, then reveal. (MyBank is a made-up bank for this example.)

  • https://www.mybank.com.my/login
    Real The owner is mybank.com.my. "www." is just a part the owner chose.
  • https://mybank.com.my.account-check.top/login
    Scam The owner is account-check.top. The bank's name at the start is a disguise.
  • https://rnybank.com.my/login
    Scam Look closely: r + n pretends to be m. A look-alike name.
  • https://bit.ly/3xYz9Qa
    Can't tell A short link hides where it really goes. Don't open it from a stranger. Paste it into the checker first.
Look-alike lettersrn for m, 0 for o, 1 for l, extra or missing letters.
Brand in the wrong placeThe brand's name early in the link, but a different owner before the first /.
Odd endingsUnusual endings such as .top, .xyz or .help on a "bank" or "delivery" site.
The padlock isn't proof🔒 only means the connection is private. Scam websites have it too.

05 Baits young people see a lot

Scammers follow the crowd. These are the stories they tell most often to students and young people. Each one has a tell — the giveaway that it's bait.

  • Games

    Free skins, coins or diamonds

    Log in with your game account to claim 5,000 free coins.

    The tell Real games give rewards inside the game, never on another website.

  • Delivery

    Parcel on hold

    Your parcel can't be delivered. Pay RM1.99 to reschedule.

    The tell A tiny fee is a trick to get your card details. Check in the courier's own app.

  • Social media

    Account will be suspended

    Your post broke copyright rules. Appeal here or lose your account.

    The tell Real warnings appear inside the app, not as a DM with a login link.

  • Jobs

    Easy money

    Earn RM300 a day just liking videos. No experience needed!

    The tell Pay that's too easy, then a "deposit" or your bank account is needed later.

  • Friends

    A friend needs help

    I sent you a 6-digit code by mistake, can you send it back?

    The tell Their account may be hacked. Call them another way. Never share an OTP.

  • Curiosity

    Shocking photo or video

    OMG is this you in this video?? 😱

    The tell The link opens a fake login page to steal your account.

06 What to do: Stop, Check, Ask

You don't have to win an argument with a scammer. You only have to not act on their message.

  1. Stop

    Don't tap, reply, scan or pay yet. A real message will still be real in ten minutes.

  2. Check

    Open the official app or type the website yourself. Read the link. Or paste it into our checker.

  3. Ask

    Show it to a parent, teacher or friend you trust. Two pairs of eyes beat one.

Never share these

  • Your passwords
  • One-time codes (OTP / TAC), even with "the bank" or a friend
  • Card numbers, CVV or online banking details
  • Photos of your IC or other ID

Already clicked? Don't panic. Act fast.

  • Change that password from the official app or website, and anywhere you reuse it.
  • Turn on two-step verification.
  • Shared bank or card details? Call your bank now, and the National Scam Response Centre on 997.
  • Tell an adult you trust, then report and block the sender.
